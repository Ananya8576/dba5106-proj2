Tree SHAP on the chosen model, the boosted trees with log loss, on the validation split. The question we most wanted answered: does the model look at the scratch? Globally, about half. The seven line features carry 49 percent of the attribution, and three of the top four features are line features: the share of failing dies in thin structures, the line score of the most line-like dies, and the number of line segments. But the single largest feature is a background one, the fail rate in the outer ring. Some of that is legitimate context, because a line stands out more on a quiet wafer. Some may be the dataset itself: which other patterns tend to come with a Scratch label. We flag that openly. Locally, two wafers chosen by rule. The wafer the model is most sure about is a scratch-only wafer with a visible diagonal line, and line features lead its explanation. The costliest mistake is a wafer labelled Donut plus Edge-Loc plus Loc plus Scratch. Large blobs cover most of the wafer and leave few thin structures, so the line evidence is swamped and the model says no scratch. That is a failure we can understand and explain to an engineer: heavy defects hide scratches. SHAP values add up exactly to the model's output.
