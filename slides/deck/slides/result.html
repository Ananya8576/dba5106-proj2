Audit of our own choice

On test, the blackbox we didn’t choose scores 0.158 against our 0.181. The difference (−0.023, 95% CI −0.051 to +0.007) isn’t distinguishable from zero. We did not switch after seeing test.

Limits

  • The 5:1 cost ratio is our assumption.
  • Test is worse than validation (0.181 vs 0.143): the optimiser’s curse.
  • Labels are engineers’ judgements; some look wrong.

What we did differently

  • Our own physical features, not a CNN.
  • Splits by lot; 24 configurations; test used once.
  • An interval on every comparison.

Final model: L1 logistic regression (log loss) + isotonic calibration, threshold 0.22. Test split: 1,758 wafers.

The test split was used once, with the model, calibrator and threshold frozen. AUC 0.958. Evaluation loss 0.181 per wafer, which is 56 percent below holding the tool for every wafer and 17 percent below using a 0.5 threshold. We catch 97.3 percent of Edge-Loc wafers. Test is worse than validation, 0.181 against 0.143. That is the optimiser's curse from the lectures: the model and threshold were both picked on validation, so the test number is the honest one. Audit: after freezing everything, we scored the blackbox we did not choose on test. It does somewhat better, 0.158 against 0.181, with 8 fewer missed wafers and the same false alarms. The difference is not distinguishable from zero, and we did not switch, because choosing after seeing test would make the test meaningless. So our choice is defensible on interpretability and on the validation evidence, but the data does not prove the glassbox is cheaper. Limits: the 5 to 1 cost ratio is assumed; the labels are human judgements. Next steps: real cost figures from a fab, and a sparser glassbox such as EBM or a shallow tree.
