We checked calibration with a reliability diagram, the expected calibration error and the Brier score, for all four models. The chosen model, boosted trees with log loss, was already close: an error of 0.011. The L2 versions are clearly worse, 0.077 for the glassbox and 0.035 for the blackbox, which is the second cost of training with L2 after the scores outside 0 to 1. The fix: Platt scaling fitted on out-of-fold training scores, so the validation split is not reused. It takes the error to 0.009 and leaves AUC unchanged. Why calibration matters for the decision: with calibrated probabilities, decision theory gives the threshold directly as 1 over 1 plus the cost ratio, which is 0.091 for our 10 to 1 ratio. Our searched threshold on calibrated probabilities is 0.105, and using the formula would cost almost the same. Across cost ratios from 1 to 20 the searched threshold stays within 0.03 of the formula, so if the costs change, the threshold can be updated by formula.
