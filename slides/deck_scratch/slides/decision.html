The decision rule was fixed before we looked at validation. An engineer has to act on a flag by holding a handler, so a model they can read is worth something. We said we would choose the blackbox only if its validation AUC was distinguishably higher and it lowered the evaluation loss by at least 10 percent. It does both: AUC 0.963 against 0.909, and a cost of 0.250 against 0.406, which is 38 percent lower. So we chose the boosted trees trained with log loss. Then the threshold. With a miss costing ten false alarms, the cost-minimising threshold is 0.117, not 0.5. That cuts the cost by 59 percent: misses fall from 414 to 51, and false alarms rise from 395 to 1,339. Accuracy falls by about 8 points while the cost falls by more than half, which is why the evaluation loss, not accuracy, should drive the decision. We also varied the cost ratio from 1 to 20: the blackbox is the cheaper model at every ratio.
